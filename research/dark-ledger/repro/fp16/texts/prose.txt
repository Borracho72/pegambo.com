The old lighthouse stood at the edge of the cliff, its white paint peeling after decades of salt wind. Every evening the keeper climbed the spiral stairs, counted the one hundred and twelve steps, and lit the lamp that warned ships away from the rocks below. Nobody in the village remembered when the keeper had first arrived. Some said he had been a sailor who lost his ship in a storm; others claimed he was a teacher from the city who wanted silence more than anything else. The children liked to watch him from the harbour wall, wondering what he wrote in the thick notebook he carried under his arm. One winter the lamp failed during the worst storm of the year, and the keeper worked through the night with frozen hands until the light returned. In the morning a fishing boat came home safely, and the whole village finally understood why he had stayed.
